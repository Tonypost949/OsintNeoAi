# THE CHRONOLOGICAL ORDER — product frame (his name, 2026-11-13)
## The board below is one view of this system.
- **Name: THE CHRONOLOGICAL ORDER.** "Our mind thinks in boards — still
  frames… still goes, here's a full-size shot of the same thing… in that
  hour… it just rolled into a timeline… those dots in chronological order."
  Dots = items; order = time; boards = still frames; any frame opens to the
  full-size shot.
- **The offer:** people log in, get **three investigations**, use all the
  tools. They type their story; the system builds an actual **wiki page** —
  the standard parts of a wiki page — out of their material, piece by piece
  as they talk. What belongs on the page goes on the page; the rest is the
  investigative side (his words: pen-testing them and their companies —
  checking what they put in: "did you do it? go through it").
- **The graph is small** (his scope rule): a person is ~two businesses, a
  spouse, a couple kids — five to seven people, three major business
  contacts. Every person laid out this way; every company the same. If
  something's wrong, it's wrong with one of those few, or in the house.
- **Records:** the material exists and is public — including the kind you
  "have to go down to the county clerk's office to get." Filed, listed, and
  (his sarcasm noted) supposedly never looked at again. This system looks.

# VISUAL CALENDAR EVIDENCE BOARD — THE SPEC (LOCKED)
## His design, stated across 2026-11-13. Written down once so it never has to be asked for again.
### Home: a view inside OsintNeoAi. Sketches: sketches/ in this folder.

## THE IDEA IN ONE LINE
People remember **dates**. Start with ONE date, show everything that exists for
that day, then move day by day — who's who and what's what assembles itself.

## THE RULES (his words, turned into build rules)
1. **Date first.** The program opens on a date — big, on screen — then the date
   fades and a **counter** starts: hours, then days, time-since, always running.
   Anchor date for this case: **August 4, 2021**.
2. **One day at a time.** You pick a day; the program checks EVERY store for
   that day only, and populates as it goes. Not a week, not a sweep — a day.
3. **Markers, never moves.** Files stay where they live. The board records and
   marks them in place. Moving files fucks things up; marking doesn't.
4. **Layers populate.** Layer 1: basic info for the day. Layer 2: the documents
   themselves. Layer 3: the pulls (what's missing, what settles it). Each layer
   lands when it's ready; people can see something is happening and steer.
5. **Summary in front, real file behind.** Every item is a card: a translucent
   summary written over the actual file. Toggle the summary OFF = full shot of
   the real file. Toggle ON = summary back. Nothing is only a summary.
6. **The sheet is the source of truth.** Under the visuals sits a plain
   spreadsheet — Excel/Google Sheets, 1996-simple, cell by cell, row by row.
   A universal table nobody can fuck up, that he can pull up anywhere and send
   anywhere. The board is a VIEW of the sheet, never a replacement for it.
7. **Show the work.** Small milestones as it populates, so the person watching
   can steer. No silent long runs, no finished-thing-out-of-nowhere.
8. **Persistence.** Saved in his environment (OsintNeoAi), versioned, never
   rebuilt from scratch. Standards over novelty: get the 2D board right;
   sketches first, then the working board, 3D later if ever.
9. **WHEN is the master axis** (his, 2026-11-13): "it's not who what where
   when why — it's WHEN. Time is the only [truth]." Every view sorts by time
   first; everything else hangs off the date.
10. **Media created that day is a layer.** Photos, video, files CREATED on
    that date — pulled day by day, so you stand in the victim's shoes and see
    what his day actually held. (Measured 2026-11-13: the downloaded album
    copies carry NO EXIF dates — creation dates live in the Google Photos
    originals. That pull is the media layer's named next step.)
11. **Real only.** No fake dark renders, no game-looking mock documents. The
    sketches are sketches; the board itself shows real files and real text or
    nothing. And it is **phone-first** — "you are a phone now": it has to work
    on his phone before it works anywhere else.
12. **Gates** (his word): "go back to the gate — that's where you start;
    everything happening on the gate." Each day is read through its gates —
    the checkpoints the day had to pass (notice served? deadline hit?
    execution done?) — the gate log is part of every day card.
