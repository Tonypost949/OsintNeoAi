## 4. THE LETTER OF THE LAW (California Penal Code — letter first, opinion after)

- **§518** — Extortion is the obtaining of property from another, with his consent, induced by a wrongful use of force or fear, or under color of official right.
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=518
- **§519** — The fear may be induced by a threat to do an unlawful injury to the person or property of the individual threatened **or of a third person** — here: his mother, his home, his business.
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=519
- **§520** — Punishment: 2, 3, or 4 years.
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=520
- **§523** — Whoever, with intent to extort property or other consideration from another, sends or delivers a letter or other writing expressing or implying a threat of a kind in §519 **shall be punished in the same manner as if the property were actually obtained**. The demand writing is its own completed offense — payment or no payment.
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=523
- **§524** — Attempted extortion is separately punishable.
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=524
- Tenant Relief Act declaration regime — the declaration the whole sequence turned on: Civ. Proc. Code §1179.01 et seq.; the §1179.04 notice was served by posting Feb 11, 2021 per the UD bundle's Exhibit 1.

## 5. THE OPINIONS — WHAT COURTS HAVE ALREADY HELD

- **People v. Beggs, 178 Cal. 79 (1918)** (California Supreme Court) — Threatening to accuse someone of a crime in order to collect a debt is extortion under §§518/519/523, **even if the debt is genuinely owed**. The lawful path to collect is civil court, not threats.
  https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/people-v-beggs
- **Flatley v. Mauro (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299** (California Supreme Court) — A demand letter plus follow-up calls demanding money constituted **criminal extortion as a matter of law** under §§518, 519 and 523, and extortionate communications are not constitutionally protected speech. A writing that demands money under threat is not a mere "settlement negotiation."
  Full opinion: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2006/s128429.html
- **People v. Massengale (1968)** (Court of Appeal) — Extortion does **not** require an explicit threat; the overall conduct and circumstances that create a reasonable fear can meet §§518/524.

**The standard the packet is built to:** proof beyond a reasonable doubt means every
element of §518/§523 met by a document, not by narration. Section 6 maps them.
