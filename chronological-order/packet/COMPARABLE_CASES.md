# COMPARABLE CASES — PEOPLE WHO DID THIS, AND WHAT HAPPENED TO THEM
## Addendum to the Extortion Packet — built 2026-11-13

Purpose (his words): cases "just like mine," with as much on each as his own file —
how they did it, what the evidence was, and how it ended — so his case can follow
the same road. Each case is labeled for what it actually is: **CONVICTION**,
**GUILTY PLEA**, **CIVIL / CITY ACTION**, or **APPELLATE STANDARD** (a court stating
the rule, not itself a conviction). Nothing here is dressed up as something it isn't.

## CASE 1 — People v. Beggs, 178 Cal. 79 (1918) — CONVICTION AFFIRMED
- **Who:** Beggs, an attorney, acting for a client (Steining) who claimed a debt.
- **How he did it:** Took the debtor, Da Rosa, to get his bank book, then to Beggs's
  office, and threatened him with a long prison term unless he paid **$2,000**.
- **Evidence:** The threat itself, made to collect money — the victim's testimony
  and the payment demand.
- **Outcome:** Jury **convicted** of extortion. The California Supreme Court
  **affirmed**: threatening to accuse someone of a crime to collect a debt is
  extortion under Penal Code §§518/519/523 **even if the debt is genuinely owed**.
- **Road for this packet:** A claimed balance is no shield. Even if every cent of
  the $5,788 had been owed, the demand-by-threat is the crime.
- Source: https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/people-v-beggs

## CASE 2 — Flatley v. Mauro (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299 — EXTORTION AS A MATTER OF LAW
- **Who:** D. Dean Mauro, an attorney, for a client claiming money.
- **How he did it:** A **demand letter** plus follow-up phone calls — pay a
  seven-figure sum (minimum $1 million demanded) or be publicly accused of rape
  and other crimes and ruined.
- **Evidence:** The letter and the calls themselves — writings and recorded
  communications, no payment ever made.
- **Outcome:** California Supreme Court: the letter and calls constituted
  **criminal extortion as a matter of law** under §§518, 519 and 523, and
  extortionate speech is **not constitutionally protected** — it cannot hide
  behind the anti-SLAPP statute or the litigation privilege at that stage.
  (Civil case — Mauro was the defendant in Flatley's suit, not a criminal
  prosecution; labeled exactly as that.)
- **Road for this packet:** A written demand is not a "negotiation." Under §523,
  the writing is punishable the same as completed extortion. His file holds the
  April 23, 2021 writing: possession or $5,788, "must be paid to stop further
  eviction proceedings."
- Source (full opinion): https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2006/s128429.html

## CASE 3 — People v. Massengale (1968), Court of Appeal — CHARGES REINSTATED
- **Who:** Three men running an aggressive home-services operation.
- **How they did it:** Went to the **homes of two elderly women**, did work, then
  presented inflated bills ($1,170 in one) with intimidating conduct and demands
  to go to the bank and pay.
- **Evidence:** The victims' testimony about the conduct and circumstances — no
  single explicit threat was required.
- **Outcome:** The trial court had dismissed the extortion information (§995);
  the Court of Appeal **reversed** — the evidence was sufficient for extortion
  charges under §§518/524 to proceed. Rule stated: extortion does not require an
  explicit threat; **overall conduct and circumstances that instill reasonable
  fear** meet the statute.
- **Road for this packet:** The porch confrontation is not saved by "nobody said
  the magic words." A surprise eviction demand, two inches from his face (his
  same-day email, Gmail `17900a0de1e68d4f`), in front of family and neighbors,
  is conduct and circumstance — graded as his account, with the email documented.
- Source: https://www.studicata.com/summaries/court-of-appeal-of-california/people-v-massengale-1968-jb8sip/

## CASE 4 — The Macys, San Francisco (2013) — LANDLORDS, GUILTY PLEAS, FELONIES
- **Who:** Kip and Nicole Macy, owners of a six-unit San Francisco apartment
  building — the press and the DA called them the "landlords from hell."
- **How they did it:** Wanted the tenants out so they could renovate and sell.
  Escalating campaign: fraudulent emails (one impersonating a tenant, one
  threatening their own attorneys), cutting utilities/services, damaging the
  building's support joists, soaking tenants' beds, clothes and electronics in
  ammonia, burglarizing units, changing locks, and threatening to shoot the
  building manager.
- **Evidence:** Tenant and manager testimony, the fraudulent emails themselves,
  physical damage.
- **Outcome:** Extradited from Italy; **pleaded guilty to four felony counts** —
  two counts of residential burglary, one of stalking, one of attempted grand
  theft — facing up to **4 years 4 months** in prison. The building was taken
  from their hands (no longer owned by them).
- **Road for this packet:** Landlords who set out to force tenants out *were*
  prosecuted as felons in California — by the San Francisco DA. The conduct
  differs (sabotage and burglary, not a demand letter), and that difference is
  stated, not hidden: this case proves the willingness to prosecute
  force-them-out landlords; Beggs/Flatley prove the demand-by-threat itself.
- Source: https://www.paloaltoonline.com/news/2013/06/20/landlords-from-hell-plead-guilty-in-four-felony-counts

## CASE 5 — Darren Stern / Landmark Equity, Los Angeles (2007–2008) — CONVICTED, THEN $10 MILLION
- **Who:** Darren Stern (aka Henry Shalom), owner of Landmark Equity Management,
  holder of 800+ rent-controlled Los Angeles apartments.
- **How he did it:** A scheme to drive low-income tenants out — squalid
  conditions and intimidation — then evict, raise rents, and sell at inflated
  profit.
- **Evidence:** The pattern across hundreds of units; city investigation.
- **Outcome:** **Convicted May 2007** (no-contest plea to nearly three dozen code
  violations; served jail time). The City of Los Angeles then sued civilly:
  **$10 million settlement** — $1 million in civil penalties and an estimated
  $9 million in restitution to former tenants, plus $105,000+ of the city's
  investigative costs.
- **Road for this packet:** The two-track road — criminal conviction first,
  city civil action with restitution second. A city attorney's office ran it.
- Source: https://www.lawyersandsettlements.com/settlements/11056/rent-controlled-appartments.html

## CASE 6 — State v. Ashley, 108 N.M. 343 (1989) — EVICTION THREAT = EXTORTION (New Mexico, labeled out-of-state)
- **Who:** Ashley, maneuvering to take over a building a tenant leased for a
  health club.
- **How he did it:** Demanded **$3,500** from the tenant; when the tenant doubted
  he could raise it, Ashley said he would buy the building anyway and the tenant
  would be **evicted and run out**. He then went to the club repeatedly telling
  staff he would soon be the boss.
- **Evidence:** The tenant's and employees' testimony about the demand and the
  threats; proof Ashley never had the money he claimed.
- **Outcome:** The court's finding that he **committed extortion** was affirmed
  (probation revoked on that basis). New Mexico's statute tracks the same
  structure as California's §519 — threat of unlawful injury to person or
  property, or to compel an act against the victim's will.
- **Road for this packet:** The closest fact-pattern in this file to a
  pay-or-be-evicted demand: money demanded, eviction threatened, extortion found.
- Source: https://nmonesource.com/nmos/nmca/en/386123/1/document.do

## THE GAP, STATED PLAINLY

In the sources searched on 2026-11-13, **no California case was found where a
landlord was criminally convicted under §518 specifically for a pay-or-leave
demand letter to a tenant.** The pieces exist separately and are all above:

- threats-to-collect = extortion, conviction affirmed (*Beggs*);
- a demand **letter** = criminal extortion as a matter of law (*Flatley*, §523);
- conduct/circumstance without explicit threats suffices (*Massengale*);
- force-tenants-out landlords prosecuted as felons (*Macy*), convicted and then
  hit for $10M civilly (*Stern*);
- pay-or-be-evicted demand found to be extortion (*Ashley*, NM).

His file is the one that puts the pieces in one stack: a written demand, a false
pretext contradicted by the demander's own paper, a disabled mother, a pandemic
relief regime, and the possession actually taken on August 4, 2021. Stated as
what it is: the road is built from these cases; the exact combined case has not
been found yet, and the search for it stays on the register as a live pull.

## THE AGGRAVATOR THE OTHER CASES DIDN'T NEED
**Penal Code §525:** upon a felony extortion conviction, the fact that the victim
was an **elder or dependent person** shall be considered a circumstance in
**aggravation** at sentencing. His disabled mother lived in that home, was named
in the porch threat ("we throw you out, your disabled mother out"), and the
demand was made in a pandemic, against a tenant paying under the documented
$500/month relief routine — not a tenant who had simply stopped paying.

### W-1 — Kevin Baker, Zanesville, Ohio — GUILTY PLEA, 2 YEARS IN PRISON (DOCUMENTED, DA source)
- **Who:** Kevin Baker, 72–73, landlord, a retired police officer.
- **What he demanded:** Sex acts from his tenant **in exchange for dropping /
  cancelling the eviction he had filed against her** in Zanesville Municipal
  Court — plus her utilities turned back on. The demands were made **in
  writing**; the tenant kept the messages. His own words, per the prosecutor:
  "Hey [victim] it's Kevin your landlord IF you would [perform a sex act] I'll
  cancel the eviction. We don't need to go to court, we can take care of this
  at my home." He also served her a "notice to leave premises" and threatened
  her against reporting him ("I'm a retired cop I got pull in this town if you
  don't do what I say I'm [not] cancelling this eviction").
- **Charge:** Extortion — the Muskingum County Prosecutor's plea release is
  headlined and framed as extortion ("admit his guilt in extorting a
  Zanesville-area tenant"; potential three-year sentence). The prosecutor's
  sentencing release records the plea count as **Intimidation, a third-degree
  felony** (Ohio). Both prints are stated here exactly as the DA published
  them.
- **Outcome:** **Guilty plea** before Judge Kelly Cottrill, Muskingum County
  Court of Common Pleas; **sentenced to 2 years in prison** (sentencing
  release dated July 27, 2026).
- **Road for this packet:** This is the proof-of-concept that a prosecutor
  *will* charge a landlord for trading on an eviction — "we don't need to go
  to court" is the same lever as "must be paid to stop further eviction
  proceedings." The difference is stated plainly: Baker's price was sex, not
  money.
- Sources (Muskingum County Prosecutor's Office):
  https://www.muskingumprosecutor.org/News-Events/Local-Landlord-Guilty-Of-Sex-Related-Racist-Extortion/398/
  https://www.muskingumprosecutor.org/News-Events/Local-Landlord-Convicted-Of-Sex-Related-Racist-Intimidation-Sentenced-2-Years-In-Prison/422/

### W-3 — The Podolskys / Roitman, New York, NY (1983–1985) — GUILTY PLEAS, FELONIES (charges DOCUMENTED, court opinion; plea/sentence details NEWS)
- **Who:** Landlords Zenek Podolsky and George Roitman, owners of three SRO
  buildings on West 77th Street, Manhattan (family members Jay and Stuart
  Podolsky in the pleas).
- **How they did it:** Hired the "Lender–Lambert gang" — paid **$500–$1,500
  per tenant driven out** — to terrorize roughly 30 low-income tenants into
  vacating: opening the buildings to drug dealers and prostitutes, cutting
  essential services, harassment and vandalism. Ten elderly tenants were
  harassed out; the contemporary reporting records one tenant suicide and one
  death from pneumonia after a winter without heat.
- **Charges:** Indictments for conspiracy, **grand larceny** (New York's
  extortion vehicle — larceny by extortion) and **coercion**. In *People v.
  Podolsky*, 496 N.Y.S.2d 619 (Sup. Ct. 1985), the court refused to dismiss
  and held the landlords could be prosecuted notwithstanding their ownership:
  the coercive scheme to strip tenants of their right of possession
  **constituted extortion/coercion as a matter of New York law**. Gang members
  pleaded guilty and became state witnesses, then were convicted/sentenced.
- **Outcome:** Jay, Stuart and Zenek Podolsky **pleaded guilty to 37
  felonies**, including grand larceny and coercion (reported 1984 pleas): Jay
  and Stuart received probation, Zenek 90 days. In the parallel civil suit,
  the 13 remaining tenants won a **$1.2 million settlement**.
- **Road for this packet:** Ownership is no shield — a New York court said so
  in print in 1985, about landlords, in an extortion frame. The lever here was
  terror, not a letter; that difference is stated, not hidden.
- Sources: *People v. Podolsky* (1985), case summary:
  https://www.studicata.com/summaries/supreme-court-of-new-york/people-v-podolsky-1985-pveqfy/
  Plea reporting (Broward.US, 2025):

### CALIFORNIA — THE RESULT OF THE WIDENED SEARCH, STATED EXACTLY
Searched this time, for California specifically: general web under multiple
formulations ("landlord convicted extortion Penal Code 518 California tenant
rent demand"; "California district attorney landlord charged extortion
tenants"; "518 landlord extortion tenant site:law.justia.com"); prosecutor
and police press-release sources as they surfaced nationally; CourtListener /
RECAP documents (the landlord-extortion returns were civil suits by tenants
and unrelated federal Hobbs Act cases — no California landlord §518 criminal
docket was among them); and Justia/Scholar-indexed case law.

**Result: still nothing.** No California case — published opinion, DA press
release, or news report — was found in which a landlord was criminally
prosecuted under Penal Code §518 for threatening a tenant with eviction to
extract money. What California sources show instead is the **civil** door:
Civil Code §1940.2(a)(2) makes it unlawful for a landlord to engage in conduct
violating §518 "for the purpose of influencing a tenant to vacate," with a
civil penalty — quoted and applied in *Erlach v. Sierra Asset Servicing, LLC*
(2014) — i.e., in California the same conduct surfaces in the records as a
tenant's civil claim, not as a DA's criminal filing.

**And the limit of the whole widened search, stated plainly:** across every
state searched, every landlord extortion prosecution found carries an added
lever beyond a money demand — sex (Baker, Zolper), a terror campaign
(Podolsky), or forgery/fraud charged under other statutes (Merryman). The one
pure "pay money or be evicted" extortion finding in this file remains
*State v. Ashley* (New Mexico, 1989, Case 6 above). A prosecution anywhere in
the U.S. built **solely** on a written pay-or-leave money demand, charged as
extortion, was **not found** in either pass. That is the documented state of
the record after searching published opinions, prosecutor releases, federal
releases, court dockets, and news archives — the absence is in the charging
record, not in the statute: *Beggs* and *Flatley* already hold that the demand
itself is the crime in California whether or not any prosecutor has yet filed
a landlord case on it.
