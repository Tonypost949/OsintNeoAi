# LIST 1 — THE LAWS THAT WERE THERE TO PROTECT HIM

**A. The COVID Tenant Relief Act (AB 3088, 2020) — Code Civ. Proc. §§1179.01–1179.07**
The regime this entire case ran under. A landlord could not evict for COVID-period
rent if the tenant delivered a signed declaration of COVID-related financial
distress. The landlord had to serve a 15-day notice with the declaration form and
could not treat the rent as a basis for eviction where the declaration was given.
*His paper:* Doyle's Feb 21, 2021 email — declaration received (E-02). The Mar 18
notice's own checked February box (E-05). His signed declaration page (E-07).

**B. The CARES Act, §4024 — 30-day notice for covered properties**
Federal law required a 30-day notice to vacate for covered dwellings. The
landlord's own notice is titled "NOTICE TO PAY RENT IN 15 DAYS OR QUIT IN 30 DAYS
**(Covered by CARES Act)**" — they papered themselves under the Act. (E-05)

**C. The CDC Eviction Moratorium (85 Fed. Reg. 55292)**
A tenant who delivered a CDC declaration could not be evicted for nonpayment.
The landlord's own UD-101 has a checkbox about the CDC statement (item 4a) — the
protection is inside their own filing. (E-14)

**D. Civil Code §1942.5 — Anti-retaliation**
A landlord may not retaliate — including by evicting or threatening to evict —
against a tenant who exercised a legal right or complained to a government
agency. *His account:* the Aug 4 entry was retaliation for his Jul 1–12
complaints to agencies (his complaint letter in Drive, read 2026-10-28).
Labeled: his account; the complaints themselves are documented.

**E. Welfare & Institutions Code §§15600 et seq. — Elder & Dependent Adult Protection**
§15610.07 defines abuse of an elder or dependent adult, including taking their
property by undue influence (§15610.70); §15657 gives a prevailing plaintiff
attorney's fees; mandated reporting §§15630–15631. *The tie:* his disabled
mother lived in that home and was named in the porch threat.
**Penal Code §525** — elder/dependent victim = aggravation at sentencing on a
felony extortion conviction.

**F. Code Civ. Proc. §715.020 — How a writ may be executed**
The sheriff must serve/post a Notice to Vacate giving the occupant at least
**5 days** before enforcement. *His paper:* posted Jul 5 for a Jul 12 deadline —
then not executed until Aug 4, three weeks later, inside the undocumented
interval (E-18, E-19, MIA-107).

**G. Code Civ. Proc. §1176 — Stay and appeal in UD cases**
A UD defendant may seek a stay of enforcement and appeal. *His paper:* his
ex parte was filed Aug 20 — after the Aug 4 execution — and the minute order
"stayed" a lockout that had already happened (E-16, MIA-110).

# LIST 2 — THE LAWS BROKEN, ON THIS PAPER

Each entry: the law, then the document that ties it. Grading stays on:
DOCUMENTED paper vs HIS ACCOUNT. Charging decisions belong to prosecutors;
this list is the stack, built to the letter of the law.

**1. Penal Code §518 / §519 — Extortion**
Obtaining property with consent induced by wrongful force or fear; fear induced
by threat of unlawful injury to the person or property of the one threatened
**or of a third person** (§519(1)).
*Tie:* the demand sequence (E-08 → E-11) and possession actually taken Aug 4
(E-18). The porch threat against him, his mother, and his business is his
account, written the same day (E-10, MIA-015).

**2. Penal Code §523 — Extortion by written threat**
Sending a writing that expresses or implies a §519 threat, with intent to
extort, is punished **as if the property were actually obtained**.
*Tie:* **DOCUMENTED** — the Apr 23, 2021 email: possession or **$5,788**,
"must be paid to stop further eviction proceedings" (E-11). The writing is in
hand. This is the cleanest count in the packet.

**3. Penal Code §520 — The punishment**
2, 3, or 4 years. §525 adds the elder/dependent aggravator (List 1E).

**4. Penal Code §§118 / 118a — Perjury**
A statement under penalty of perjury, willfully false, in a matter material.
*Ties, all paper:* (a) UD-101 item 7d(2), verified by Nunez May 15, 2021,
alleges he did **not** deliver a declaration **in the time required** — against the office's own Feb 21
receipt email and the notice's checked box (E-02, E-05, E-14). (b) UD-120,
signed by Nunez Nov 27, 2021: no rental assistance received, none pending —
testable against relief-program payment records (E-23; pull named in the
Register). (c) The §170.6 declaration conflict (MIA-110) — jurat status NOT
YET DOCUMENTED; settles with the certified record. *Stated as sworn paper in
conflict with the swearer's own documents; prosecution is the DA's call.*

**5. The Tenant Relief Act's own rules (List 1A) — broken inside the UD**
Filing a UD on COVID-period rent where a declaration was delivered is exactly
what §§1179.02–1179.03 barred. The UD was filed May 18, 2021 anyway (E-15),
on the sworn claim that no declaration was delivered in the time required (E-14).

**6. Civil Code §789.3 — Interference with a tenancy**
A landlord may not act to force a tenant out outside the court process —
and even the court process was bent after judgment: the Sheriff's form gave
**15 days** to recover his property; the landlord's email cut it to **the end
of one business day** (E-20 vs E-21). DOCUMENTED, both writings in hand.

# LIST 3 — WHO ALREADY LOST AN EXTORTION CASE OR SUIT
*(Full files in COMPARABLE_CASES.md — these are the losers.)*

| Who | How they lost |
|---|---|
| **Beggs** (attorney, 1918) | Jury convicted him of extortion; CA Supreme Court affirmed. Threats to collect a debt = extortion, owed or not. |
| **Mauro** (attorney, 2006) | CA Supreme Court held his demand letter + calls were criminal extortion **as a matter of law**; his anti-SLAPP shield was struck down. He lost the "it's just a demand letter" defense forever. |
| **Massengale defendants** (1968) | Won dismissal below, **lost on appeal** — extortion charges reinstated on conduct-and-fear evidence alone. |
| **Kip & Nicole Macy** (landlords, SF, 2013) | **Pleaded guilty to four felonies** for forcing tenants out; faced 4 years 4 months. |
| **Darren Stern** (landlord, LA, 2007–08) | **Convicted**, jailed, then paid **$10 million** to the city and his former tenants. |
| **Ashley** (NM, 1989) | Extortion finding affirmed — pay-or-be-evicted was the threat that did it. |

**The defense that always gets raised — and why it loses here:** "claim of
right" / "good-faith debt collection." *Beggs* killed it for threats in 1918;
*Flatley* killed it for demand letters in 2006. A debt, even a real one, is
collected in court — not by threatening a man, his disabled mother, and his
business on his porch in a pandemic, and not by a writing that says
possession or $5,788.
