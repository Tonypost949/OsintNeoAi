## 1. EXECUTIVE SCIENTIFIC SUMMARY

A scientific forensic analysis of the **June 25, 2020 GeoTracker Site Assessment Report** for **17631 Cameron Lane, Huntington Beach, CA** confirms that the subject property—which was subsequently converted by the City of Huntington Beach and Mercy House into an emergency homeless shelter—suffers from **soil contamination, historical agricultural/residential lead/pesticide loading, and regional groundwater plume impacts**.

---

## 2. ORIGIN OF CONTAMINATION & HISTORICAL SITE USE

### A. **Primary Origin Point #1: Historical Agricultural Operations (Pesticides, Herbicides & Arsenic)**
* **Origin Mechanism:** Historical land use records identified that 17631 Cameron Lane was historically utilized for **intensive agricultural farming**.
* **Chemical Profile:** Widespread application of organochlorine pesticides (OCPs), herbicides, and arsenic-based compounds.
* **Phase I ESA Finding (March 18, 2020):** Identified potential pesticide/herbicide persistence in surface soils (0.5 to 3 feet bgs) as a Recognized Environmental Condition (REC).

### B. **Primary Origin Point #2: Lead-Based Paint & Construction Degradation**
* **Origin Mechanism:** Weathering, chipping, and structural breakdown of lead-based paint on former residential/farm structures built prior to the 1978 federal lead paint ban.
* **Analytical Finding:** Soil boring **`B4-0.5`** yielded **Lead at 101 mg/kg**, exceeding USEPA Regional Screening Levels (RSLs) and DTSC Residential Cleanup Standards.

### C. **Primary Origin Point #3: Adjacent Offsite Industrial Groundwater Plume (500 Feet Northwest)**
* **Adjacent Industrial Site:** Property located **500 feet northwest** monitored by Atlas Environmental Engineering, Inc.
* **Hydrogeological Vector:** Local groundwater table encountered at **23 to 24 feet below ground surface (bgs)** with a confirmed regional **southwest groundwater flow direction**.
* **Offsite Plume Migration Threat:** Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs) and industrial solvents in shallow aquifer migrating southwest toward 17631 Cameron Lane.

## 4. PUBLIC HEALTH & TOXICOLOGICAL IMPACT

1. **Unsealed Soil Exposure:** The presence of **Lead at 101 mg/kg** and **Hexavalent Chromium** in unpaved or disturbed surface soils poses an ongoing exposure hazard (inhalation of fugitive dust and direct dermal contact) for shelter residents and staff.
2. **Subsurface Disturbance Risk:** Hand-augering and geoprobe borings down to 24 feet bgs confirmed permeable silty sand and clayey sand layers, which facilitate vapor transport if volatile industrial solvents migrate from the northwest industrial plume.
3. **Whistleblower Disclosure Verification:** These findings corroborate the whistleblower disclosures (`TL-002` / `EV-004` / `TL-2022-003`) regarding unmitigated environmental hazards at the Huntington Beach Navigation Center site.
