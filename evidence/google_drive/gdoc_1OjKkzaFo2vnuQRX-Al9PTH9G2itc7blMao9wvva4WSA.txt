The collapse of this specific tier of the network occurred when federal authorities intervened. Part II: The CalAIM Per-Capita Billing Fraud and The "HIPAA Harvest"

The architecture of this secondary fraud is structural, leveraging the very mechanisms designed to provide care in order to generate perpetual revenue streams without delivering meaningful services.
CalAIM represents a massive reform of the Medi-Cal system, shifting from a traditional fee-for-service model to a managed care framework. Under this system, contracted nonprofits are compensated on a per-client, per-month basis for enrolling vulnerable individuals into Enhanced Care Management (ECM) and Community Supports programs. The financial incentives embedded within this system heavily favor the enrollment of high-acuity individuals. Children and youth are designated as a specific "Population of Focus" under CalAIM, carrying higher intensity tiers and significantly higher reimbursement ceilings.
The structural flaw actively exploited by the network is that operators do not necessarily need to provide meaningful, outcome-driven services to generate revenue; they simply need to acquire the necessary clinical documentation to trigger and maintain the enrollment of the client. This creates a perverse "per-capita capture economy" where achieving housing stability and family preservation actively eliminates the revenue stream. The network operates on a documented financial incentive to separate individuals, particularly children, from their families and support systems. A single child placed in the foster or ward-of-state system generates approximately $250,000 annually through bundled Title IV-E and CalAIM pediatric billing, compared to a maximum of approximately $65,000 annually for an adult enrolled in Tier 1 ECM.

This regulatory action was legally ultra vires, meaning it was performed entirely beyond the agency's statutory authority.
