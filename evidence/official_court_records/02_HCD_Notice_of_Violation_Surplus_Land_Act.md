## 2. STATUTORY FRAMEWORK & VERBATIM LEGISLATIVE CITATIONS

### A. Legislative Intent & Purpose (Cal. Gov. Code § 54220)
California Government Code § 54220 establishes the paramount state priority of utilizing surplus public land to alleviate the statewide affordable housing shortage:
> **Cal. Gov. Code § 54220(a):**  
> *"The Legislature reaffirms its declaration that housing is of vital statewide importance to the health, safety, and welfare of the residents of this state and that the provision of a decent home and a suitable living environment for every Californian is a priority of the highest order. The Legislature further declares that there is a severe shortage of affordable housing in California and that the disposal of surplus land, or land that is not necessary for the agency's use, by local agencies should prioritize the development of low- and moderate-income housing."*

### B. Mandatory Declaration of Surplus Land (Cal. Gov. Code § 54221)
Assembly Bill 1486 enacted strict pre-disposition requirements eliminating backdoor exemptions:
> **Cal. Gov. Code § 54221(b)(1):**  
> *"Except as provided in Section 54222.3, prior to taking any action to dispose of real property, a local agency shall take formal action in a regular public meeting declaring that the land is surplus land or exempt surplus land, as supported by written findings."*
> 
> **Cal. Gov. Code § 54221(c):**  
> *"'Dispose of real property' means the sale, lease for more than 15 years, or other transfer of real property by a local agency."*

### C. Written Notice of Availability (NOA) Requirement (Cal. Gov. Code § 54222)
> **Cal. Gov. Code § 54222:**  
> *"Any local agency disposing of surplus land shall send, prior to taking any action to dispose of the property, a written notice of availability of the property to all of the following:*  
> *(a) A local public entity within whose jurisdiction the surplus land is located. Housing sponsors, as defined by Section 50074 of the Health and Safety Code, that have notified the department of their interest in participating in the Surplus Land Act.*  
> *(b) For the purpose of developing park or recreational facilities: To any park or recreation department of any city or county within which the land is situated...*  
> *(c) For the purpose of developing open space: To the regional open space authority...*  
> *(d) To any school district within whose jurisdiction the surplus land is located."*

### D. 60-Day Notice & 90-Day Good Faith Negotiations (Cal. Gov. Code § 54223)
> **Cal. Gov. Code § 54223(a):**  
> *"After the disposing agency has sent written notice to all entities specified in Section 54222, those entities shall have 60 days to submit a notice of interest to the disposing agency... The disposing agency shall enter into good faith negotiations to determine a mutually agreeable sales price or lease terms with the entity for not less than 90 days."*

### E. Rejection of Grandfathering / Retroactive Exemptions (Cal. Gov. Code § 54234)
Under AB 1486, the Legislature established narrow transition rules for pre-existing agreements:
> **Cal. Gov. Code § 54234(a)(1):**  
> *"The disposition of surplus land shall not be subject to this article if the local agency entered into an exclusive negotiating agreement or legally binding agreement to dispose of the property prior to September 30, 2019, provided that the disposition is completed not later than December 31, 2022."*

### F. Mandatory Statutory Civil Penalty (Cal. Gov. Code § 54230.5)
Section 54230.5 establishes a non-discretionary, severe financial penalty for municipalities that violate the SLA:
> **Cal. Gov. Code § 54230.5(a)(1):**  
> *"A local agency that disposes of land in violation of this article after receiving a notification from the department pursuant to subdivision (b) shall be liable for a civil penalty calculated as follows:*  
> *(A) For a first violation, 30 percent of the final sales price of the land sold in violation of this article.*  
> *(B) For a second or subsequent violation, 50 percent of the final sales price of the land sold in violation of this article."*
> 
> **Cal. Gov. Code § 54230.5(b):**  
> *"Prior to submitting a civil penalty to the local agency, the department shall provide the local agency with a written notification that the local agency is in violation of this article. The local agency shall have 60 days to cure the violation."*

## 3. FINDINGS OF FACT & SUBSTANTIVE STATUTORY VIOLATIONS

The December 8, 2021 Notice of Violation issued by HCD Deputy Director Megan Kirkeby set forth comprehensive findings of non-compliance across four primary operational areas:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                           CALIFORNIA HCD SLA VIOLATION ARCHITECTURE                              |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. FAILURE TO DECLARE SURPLUS    |  City executed DDA on Dec 20, 2019 without holding public     |
|     (Cal. Gov. Code § 54221)      |  hearing or adopting formal resolution declaring 150 acres   |
|                                   |  "surplus land" or "exempt surplus land."                     |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
|  2. FAILURE TO ISSUE NOA          |  City engaged in private bilateral negotiations with SRB      |
|     (Cal. Gov. Code § 54222)      |  Management Co. LLC without sending written NOA to certified  |
|                                   |  housing sponsors, park districts, or school entities.        |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
|  3. REJECTION OF EXEMPTION DEFENSE|  City claimed 1996 Angel Stadium lease created pre-existing   |
|     (Cal. Gov. Code § 54234)      |  vested rights. HCD ruled 2019 DDA was a brand-new sale to a  |
|                                   |  new entity (SRB Management), outside § 54234 grandfathering. |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
|  4. MANDATORY 30% CIVIL PENALTY   |  Disposition of $320,000,000 parcel triggers mandatory 30%    |
|     (Cal. Gov. Code § 54230.5)    |  civil fine under § 54230.5(a)(1) = $96,000,000.00.           |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Detailed Analysis of Violations:

1. **Failure to Declare Land Surplus (Cal. Gov. Code § 54221(b)(1)):**
   * *Finding:* Anaheim City Council approved the original disposition and development agreement on December 20, 2019, and the amended and restated agreement on September 29, 2020. At no point prior to these legislative actions did the City Council hold a public hearing or adopt a resolution with written evidentiary findings declaring the 150-acre Angel Stadium property surplus.
   * *Statutory Effect:* The disposition process was legally void *ab initio* due to the absence of the foundational jurisdictional prerequisite mandated by § 54221.

2. **Bypassing the Notice of Availability (NOA) (Cal. Gov. Code § 54222):**
   * *Finding:* Anaheim never issued a written Notice of Availability to HCD-registered housing sponsors, the Orange County Housing Authority, the local school district, or regional park agencies.
   * *Statutory Effect:* Affordable housing developers were entirely excluded from proposing 100% affordable or mixed-income residential projects on the 150-acre Platinum Triangle parcel, directly violating the legislative mandate of AB 1486.

3. **Rejection of the "1996 Lease Rights" Grandfathering Defense (Cal. Gov. Code § 54234):**
   * *Anaheim Defense:* In formal letters dated May 14, 2021, and November 2, 2021, the Anaheim City Attorney argued that the transaction was exempt under § 54234 because the 1996 Stadium Lease between the City and Angels Baseball LP granted the team long-term leasehold and development options that pre-dated September 30, 2019.
   * *HCD Determination:* HCD unequivocally rejected Anaheim's defense:
     * The 2019/2020 Purchase and Sale Agreement was executed with **SRB Management Co. LLC**, an entity formed in November 2019, which was legally separate and distinct from Angels Baseball LP.
     * The transaction transferred fee title ownership of 150.1 acres, whereas the 1996 agreement was a limited leasehold covering stadium operations and surface parking.
     * The City Council entered into the DDA on December 20, 2019—*after* the September 30, 2019 grandfathering cutoff set by Cal. Gov. Code § 54234(a)(1).

## 4. MATHEMATICAL COMPUTATION OF STATUTORY CIVIL PENALTY

Pursuant to California Government Code § 54230.5(a)(1)(A), HCD calculated the mandatory statutory civil penalty based on the gross sales price established in the September 29, 2020 Amended Purchase and Sale Agreement:

$$\text{Gross Contract Sales Price} = \$320,000,000.00$$
$$\text{Statutory Penalty Rate (First Violation, § 54230.5(a)(1)(A))} = 30\%$$
$$\mathbf{\text{Mandatory Civil Penalty}} = \$320,000,000.00 \times 0.30 = \mathbf{\$96,000,000.00}$$

### Breakdown of Contract Valuation & Discount Credits:
* **Contract Baseline Gross Value:** $320,000,000.00
* **Developer Community Benefit Credits Deducted by City:**
  * Subsidized Affordable Housing Credit (466 on-site units): -$123,700,000.00
  * 7-Acre Public Park Construction Credit: -$46,200,000.00
* **Net Cash Consideration Payable to City:** $150,100,000.00
* **Statutory Assessment Rule:** Section 54230.5 applies the 30% penalty to the **final gross sales price ($320M)**, not the discounted cash amount ($150.1M). Consequently, the statutory penalty equaled **$96,000,000.00**—representing 64% of the entire net cash Anaheim expected to receive.

## 6. STATE LITIGATION, STIPULATED SETTLEMENT & COLLAPSE

### A. Orange County Superior Court Enforcement Action
* **Action:** *State of California ex rel. Department of Housing and Community Development v. City of Anaheim*
* **Court:** Superior Court of California, County of Orange (Central Justice Center)
* **Case Number:** `30-2020-01131102-CU-MC-CJC`
* **Intervention by Attorney General:** In April 2022, California Attorney General Rob Bonta filed a petition for writ of mandate and proposed stipulated judgment.

### B. Proposed Stipulated Settlement Terms (April 2022)
Under the proposed settlement negotiated by Attorney General Bonta and City Attorney Robert Fabela:
1. Anaheim agreed to allocate **$96,000,000.00** toward local affordable housing over a 5-year period ($45,000,000.00 into a dedicated municipal housing trust fund + $51,000,000.00 dedicated credit).
2. The State agreed to deem the SLA violation resolved, permitting the $320M stadium land sale to SRB Management to close.

### C. Emergency AG Stay & Termination (May 16–24, 2022)
1. **May 16, 2022:** Upon being briefed by the Federal Bureau of Investigation regarding the unsealing of the search warrant affidavit in *USA v. Harry Sidhu* (Case No. `8:22-mj-00185`), Attorney General Rob Bonta filed an emergency motion to stay the stipulated judgment for 60 days.
2. **May 23, 2022:** Whistleblower transmitted the comprehensive evidentiary dossier (*"Shea, The Angels, Moreno and Roundtree"*) to 40+ agencies.
3. **May 24, 2022:** The Anaheim City Council enacted **Resolution No. 2022-064**, unanimously voting to formally void and terminate the DDA and Purchase & Sale Agreement with SRB Management Co. LLC.
4. **May 27, 2022:** The Superior Court action was formally dismissed as moot following the total cancellation of the land disposition.
