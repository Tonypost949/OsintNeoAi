### 2.1 California Codes & Statutes
1. **Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 170.6 (Peremptory Disqualification of Judicial Officer):**
   * Precludes the filing of a peremptory challenge after the judicial officer has conducted a hearing or ruled on any contested issue of law or fact, or where the motion is exercised tactically to defeat a pending stay order.
2. **Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 415.45 (Service of Summons in Unlawful Detainer by Posting):**
   * Strictly governs the conditions precedent for court authorization to post summons on real property. Requires rigorous showing of reasonable diligence prior to application.
3. **Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 473(d) (Court Power to Correct or Set Aside Void Judgments):**
   * Authorizes the court, on notice or sua sponte, to set aside any void judgment or order at any time without limitation.
4. **Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 585(a) & § 585(d) (Clerk's and Court Default Judgments):**
   * Establishes the procedural prerequisites and evidentiary standards for entry of default judgments by the clerk (§ 585(a)) or by the court upon written declarations under penalty of perjury (§ 585(d)).
5. **Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 1169 (Default Entry in Summary Unlawful Detainer Actions):**
   * Regulates clerk default entries specifically in summary possession proceedings.
6. **COVID-19 Tenant Relief Act (AB 3088, SB 91, Cal. CCP § 1179.01 et seq.):**
   * Required mandatory rental assistance verification filings (Form UD-101) and protected residential tenants from non-payment evictions during declared emergency moratorium periods.
7. **California Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (AB 1482; Cal. Civil Code § 1946.2):**
   * Mandates just-cause eviction grounds, mandatory statutory cure notices, and statutory relocation assistance.

### 2.2 Controlling California Appellate & Supreme Court Precedents
* ***Rochin v. Pat Johnson Manufacturing Co.* (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1228, 1237:**
  > *"A court may not enter a second judgment where a prior judgment has not been vacated. A second judgment entered while the first judgment remains in full force and effect is void on its face."*
* ***Heidary v. Yadollahi* (2002) 99 Cal.App.4th 857, 862:**
  > *"It is fundamental that a court lacks jurisdiction to render a second judgment on identical claims against the same defendant where an earlier valid judgment has disposed of the action and has not been set aside."*
* ***Passavanti v. Williams* (1990) 225 Cal.App.3d 1602, 1606:**
  > *"There can be only one final judgment in an action... an amended or second judgment entered without vacating the first is a nullity."*
* ***Brown v. Superior Court* (1966) 242 Cal.App.2d 519, 526 & *Solberg v. Superior Court* (1977) 19 Cal.3d 182, 197:**
  > *"The right to exercise a peremptory challenge under Code of Civil Procedure section 170.6 must be exercised before a judicial determination has been rendered; invoking section 170.6 following an adverse interim stay ruling constitutes bad-faith judge shopping and is statutorily invalid."*
* ***The Swahn Group, Inc. v. Segal* (2013) 216 Cal.App.4th 696:**
  > *"Where a default judgment is entered in excess of statutory authority or without jurisdiction, it is void ab initio and subject to collateral attack at any time."*

### 4.2 Jurisdictional Voidness as a Matter of Law (*Rochin* & *Heidary*)
The entry of multiple final judgments in a single civil action represents a fundamental jurisdictional failure that renders Default Judgments #2 and #3 void ab initio:

1. **The "One Final Judgment" Rule:**
   * Under longstanding California law, there can be only one final judgment in a single lawsuit (*Passavanti v. Williams* (1990) 225 Cal.App.3d 1602, 1606). Once the Clerk of the Court entered Default Judgment #1 on June 29, 2021 (ROA #25, #26), the trial court was completely divested of subject matter jurisdiction to enter subsequent judgments unless and until the initial judgment was formally vacated pursuant to a statutory motion under Cal. CCP § 473, a motion for new trial, or a successful appeal.
2. **Absolute Lack of Jurisdiction to Enter Successive Judgments:**
   * In *Rochin v. Pat Johnson Manufacturing Co.* (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1228, 1237, the California Court of Appeal unequivocally held:
     > *"A court may not enter a second judgment where a prior judgment has not been vacated. A second judgment entered while the first judgment remains in full force and effect is void on its face."*
   * Similarly, in *Heidary v. Yadollahi* (2002) 99 Cal.App.4th 857, 862, the Court affirmed:
     > *"The trial court has no jurisdiction to enter a second default judgment while an earlier default judgment remains valid and unvacated. The second judgment is a nullity."*
3. **Application to the Docket of Case No. 30-2021-01201327-CL-UD-CJC:**
   * At no point between June 29, 2021 and February 4, 2022 did the Superior Court vacate, recall, or set aside Default Judgment #1. In fact, on August 23, 2021, the Court explicitly **denied** Defendant's motion to vacate Default Judgment #1 (ROA #38).
   * Consequently, when Plaintiff's counsel submitted a second Application for Court Default Judgment on December 3, 2021 (ROA #45, #48) and the Court entered Judgment on December 22, 2021 (ROA #50, #51), the Court acted in total excess of jurisdiction.
   * When Plaintiff's counsel inexplicably filed a third Application for Court Default Judgment on January 4, 2022 (ROA #54, #58) and the Court entered Judgment on February 4, 2022 (ROA #59, #60), this third judgment was equally void on the face of the judgment roll.

### 5.2 Legal & Statutory Violation of Cal. CCP § 170.6
1. **Untimeliness After Determination of Contested Substantive Rights:**
   * Under California Code of Civil Procedure § 170.6(a)(2), a peremptory challenge must be filed prior to the commencement of any hearing or the making of any substantive ruling affecting the rights of the parties.
   * Issuing an affirmative emergency stay of execution halting an eviction lockout constitutes an exercise of substantive judicial power. Filing a § 170.6 strike 78 minutes after receiving notice of an unfavorable stay order violates the strict temporal limitations established by the California Supreme Court in *Solberg v. Superior Court* (1977) 19 Cal.3d 182 and *Brown v. Superior Court* (1966) 242 Cal.App.2d 519.
2. **Bad-Faith "Judge Shopping":**
   * Disqualifying the assigned judicial officer immediately upon the entry of an emergency stay order is a classic indicium of bad-faith forum manipulation designed to coerce the transfer of an emergency proceeding to an alternate department.
