## II. THE PREDICATE ACT: SWRCB EROSIVITY WAIVER FRAUD
To circumvent the municipal grading permit process—which would have mandatorily triggered a California DTSC hazardous waste review—the developers utilized a fraudulent regulatory bypass.

* **The Mechanism:** Defendants obtained a short-term **Erosivity Waiver (WDID No. 8 30W004769)** from the State Water Resources Control Board (SWRCB).
* **The Engineer:** Hydrology and Hydraulics Analysis was drafted by **Michael Baker International** in August 2020.
* **The Fraud:** Erosivity Waivers are strictly intended for short-term, low-impact surface projects. By falsely classifying the permanent 2,247 cubic foot subterranean StormTech installation as a "temporary 90-day emergency shelter," the developers abused this waiver to illegally excavate the trench.

* **The Danger:** Unpermitted excavation directly disturbed this plume, mobilizing hazardous waste without a required Cradle-to-Grave Waste Manifest (RCRA violation).
* **The Waiver:** OCHCA Program Manager Anthony Martinez further abetted this by issuing a waiver on August 21, 2020, declaring a formal well-destruction permit was "not necessary."

The deliberate substitution of a short-term SWRCB Erosivity Waiver for a mandatory municipal grading permit constitutes intentional regulatory evasion. The unmanifested excavation of a 980 µg/kg Cr-VI plume substantiates a direct violation of the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA) and constitutes actionable, documented evidence of fraud under 31 U.S.C. § 3729 (False Claims Act).
