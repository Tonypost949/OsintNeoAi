# The Bergson Metric — measuring companies & nonprofits

Source: Henri Bergson (Nobel Prize in Literature, 1927), shared by the user 2026-11-23:
"An increase in the material means at the disposal of humanity may even present dangers unless it is accompanied by a corresponding spiritual effort."

## The equation

    β = S / M

- **M** = Material Means — what the entity has: revenue, assets, headcount, reach, technology, data.
- **S** = Spiritual Effort — what the entity actually puts toward people: mission spend, public benefit, service delivered, transparency, care.
- **β** = the Bergson ratio.

## Reading it

- **β ≥ 1** — balanced. Spiritual effort matches material means. Safe.
- **β < 1** — danger zone. The gap is the danger itself:

    D = M − S        (Danger = means not matched by effort)

## Growth form (the quote is about increase)

    ΔS ≥ ΔM

Spiritual effort must grow at least as fast as material means. Any period where ΔM > ΔS adds to D.

## Scoring an entity (0–10 each, documented figures only)

- M score: size of means — revenue/assets/reach, scaled 0–10.
- S score: share of those means verifiably devoted to the stated mission / public benefit — program-spend ratio, people served per dollar, openness of records — scaled 0–10.
- β = S ÷ M. Rank by β, flag every β < 1 with its D.
