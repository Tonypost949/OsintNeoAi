- In Fiscal Year 2019/2020, the Huntington Beach Housing Authority received a cash deposit exceeding $6,500,000.00 resulting from the re-syndication of a Low Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) development.

- To prevent this forfeiture, the City Council and Housing Authority executed a capital-parking maneuver: drawing $6,094,847.00 from LMIHAF to purchase 17631 Cameron Lane and 17642 Beach Boulevard under the pretext of "future affordable housing development."

### 4.2 HUD Emergency Solutions Grants (ESG / CARES Act ESG-CV)

- **Non-Waivable Habitability Mandate (24 CFR § 576.403(b)):**

## 7. FALSE CLAIMS ACT (31 U.S.C. § 3729) STATUTORY LIABILITY ANALYSIS

The flow of federal grant funds (HUD ESG-CV, CDBG, HOME) into the Huntington Beach Navigation Center directly triggers liability under all four core liability prongs of the federal False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. § 3729).

### 7.1 Prong 1: Presenting False Claims (31 U.S.C. § 3729(a)(1)(A))
- **Statutory Text:** Any person who "knowingly presents, or causes to be presented, a false or fraudulent claim for payment or approval" is liable to the United States Government.
- **Application — Implied False Certification Doctrine:**
  Under *Universal Health Services, Inc. v. United States ex rel. Escobar*, 579 U.S. 176 (2016), liability attaches when: (1) the claim for payment makes specific representations about the goods or services provided; and (2) the defendant's failure to disclose noncompliance with material statutory, regulatory, or contractual requirements makes those representations misleading half-truths.
- **Material Representations:**
  The City of Huntington Beach and Mercy House Living Centers, Inc. submitted monthly payment claims and drawdowns through HUD IDIS for emergency shelter operation and homeless navigation. In doing so, they explicitly represented that they were operating an eligible "Emergency Shelter" conforming to 24 CFR Part 576.
- **Material Omission:**
  Defendants knowingly concealed that the shelter was erected over an active 49x Hexavalent Chromium plume and unsealed Well W-4150, violating mandatory minimum habitability standards under 24 CFR § 576.403(b)(13). Compliance with habitability standards is a fundamental condition of payment under the McKinney-Vento Act. Had HUD known that the shelter actively exposed residents to lethal carcinogens causing chemical pneumonitis, HUD would have refused to disburse the funds. Each submitted reimbursement voucher constituted an actionable false claim.

### 7.2 Prong 2: False Records Material to a False Claim (31 U.S.C. § 3729(a)(1)(B))
- **Statutory Text:** Any person who "knowingly makes, uses, or causes to be made or used, a false record or statement material to a false or fraudulent claim" is liable.
- **Application — Fraudulent Administrative Records:**
  Defendants created and deployed a series of false records material to the receipt of federal funds:
