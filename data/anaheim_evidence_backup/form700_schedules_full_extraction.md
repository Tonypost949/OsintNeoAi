### 1.2 California Political Reform Act Disclosure Obligations
Under the California Political Reform Act of 1974 (California Government Code § 81000 et seq.), public officials managing public investments and municipal assets are required to file an annual Statement of Economic Interests (FPPC Form 700) pursuant to California Government Code § 87200. Filers must fully disclose:
- **Schedule A-1:** Investments in business entities (stocks, bonds, partnership interests) holding less than 10% equity where the entity operates or owns real property within the jurisdiction.
- **Schedule A-2:** Investments in business entities and trusts holding 10% or greater equity, including business positions, gross income, and general descriptions of business activities.
- **Schedule B:** Real property interests within the jurisdiction, or within two miles of the jurisdiction boundaries, valued at $2,000 or more, including leasehold interests and options.
- **Schedule C:** Income, loans, and business positions, including salaries, spousal income, consulting fees, and promises of economic remuneration.
- **Schedule D:** Income from gifts (meals, tickets, event sponsorships, lodging, honoraria) aggregating $50 or more from a single source, subject to the annual statutory gift limit ($470 in 2018; $500 in 2019-2020; $520 in 2021-2022).
- **Schedule E:** Travel payments, advances, and reimbursements provided by outside commercial entities, non-profits, or trade associations.

Failure to disclose material economic interests, or participating in decisions where an official maintains a disqualifying financial interest, constitutes a direct violation of California Government Code § 87100, subjecting filers to civil, administrative, and criminal penalties.
