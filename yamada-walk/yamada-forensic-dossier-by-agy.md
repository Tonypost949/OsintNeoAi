1. **Sovereign Mexican Grant (Rancho Las Bolsas)**:
   - On **May 22, 1834**, Mexican Governor José Figueroa partitioned the vast Spanish Rancho Los Nietos concession, granting **Rancho Las Bolsas** (33,460.03 acres) to **Catarina Ruiz** (widow of José Antonio Yorba). Preserved in California State Archives Spanish/Mexican Land Grant Records, File `38217`.
2. **Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo & Federal Land Patents**:
   - Following the Mexican-American War and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848), claimants petitioned the U.S. Board of Land Commissioners under the California Land Act of March 3, 1851 (Case No. `280` / `105 SD`).
   - The United States government issued two distinct sovereign land patents confirming fee title:
     - **Patent 1 (June 19, 1874)**: General Land Office (GLO) Volume 23, Pages 1–38 (Accession No. `CA0750__.001`), granting an undivided 1/2 interest in 33,460.03 acres to Ramón Yorba, Domingo Yorba, Juan Avila, et al.
     - **Patent 2 (August 27, 1877)**: GLO Volume 24, Pages 412–450 (Accession No. `CA0780__.042`), granting the remaining undivided 1/2 interest to Maria Cleofas Nieto Murillo and Juan Jose Murillo, signed by President Rutherford B. Hayes.
3. **1868 Stearns Ranchos Syndicate Survey**:
   - Don Abel Stearns acquired extensive interests across Rancho Las Bolsas. In **1868**, renowned civil engineer George Hansen surveyed and subdivided the rancho into standard one-mile square section blocks (*Map of the Stearns Ranchos*, recorded in Los Angeles County Book 1 of Maps, Page 32; Book 2 of Miscellaneous Records, Page 218). This survey formally established **Section 35, Township 5 South, Range 11 West, San Bernardino Base & Meridian (SBBM)**.
4. **1877–1879 Judicial Partition Decree**:
   - Gospel Swamp squatter conflicts and multiple ownership claims led to a judicial partition in the Los Angeles County Superior Court: *Stearns v. Cota* / *Robinson v. Stearns* (Case No. `2383`). Court referees subdivided Section 35 into farm parcels among Stearns Ranchos syndicate trustees (Alfred Robinson, Trustee) and early homesteaders.
   - Upon the formation of Orange County on **March 11, 1889**, all cadastral maps, deeds, and decrees were transcribed into Orange County Clerk-Recorder archives.

### 3.3 California Government Code § 1090 & *Thomson v. Call* (1985) 38 Cal.3d 633 Void Ab Initio Analysis

#### Statutory Framework of California Government Code § 1090
California Government Code Section 1090(a) codifies the strict common law prohibition against conflicts of interest by public officials:

#### Controlling Supreme Court Precedent: *Thomson v. Call* (1985) 38 Cal.3d 633
In the landmark decision ***Thomson v. Call* (1985) 38 Cal.3d 633**, the California Supreme Court established the absolute, draconian civil remedies governing contracts executed in violation of Section 1090:
1. **Void Ab Initio**: Contracts made in violation of Section 1090 are void from inception—not merely voidable (*Thomson*, 38 Cal.3d at 646).
2. **Good Faith Irrelevant**: Ignorance of the law, absence of corrupt intent, reliance on advice of counsel, and fair market value pricing are completely irrelevant and provide **zero defense** (*Thomson*, 38 Cal.3d at 648–649).
3. **Mandatory 100% Disgorgement Without Restitution / Offset**: The public agency is legally obligated to **retain the real property AND recover 100% of the public funds paid**, while the vendor receives nothing and is barred from seeking equitable restitution (*Thomson*, 38 Cal.3d at 646–652).
