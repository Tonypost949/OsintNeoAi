**Classification:** Working Hypothesis / Investigative Synthesis

### I. CORE PREMISE: MUTUAL INSTITUTIONAL INCENTIVES

The working hypothesis proposes that institutional frameworks across prosecution, defense, and healthcare systems operate under shared structural incentives to avoid deep, systemic scrutiny of pharmaceutical polypharmacy, adverse drug reactions, and institutional prescribing standards:

* **Scope of Exposure:** The perspective suggests that a full public and legal reckoning regarding medication-induced toxicity, rapid polypharmacy stacking, and institutional liability could create unprecedented financial and regulatory exposure for large healthcare conglomerates and pharmaceutical supply chains.
* **Broad Systemic Dimensions:** Under this viewpoint, scrutiny of medical oversight, diagnostic labeling, and institutional confinement extends beyond individual cases to broader vulnerabilities affecting vulnerable populations—including the disabled, homeless, and marginalized groups.

---

### II. ROLE ALLOCATION & STRUCTURAL DEFLECTION

* **Individual Buffers:** The hypothesis posits that individual figures (such as designated evaluators, expert witnesses, or specific prescribers) absorb localized scrutiny, functioning as focal points that insulate broader corporate healthcare systems, institutional boards, and regulatory oversight bodies from widespread discovery.
* **Adversarial Constraint:** The theory views standard legal proceedings as operating within tightly bounded parameters designed to isolate cases to individual pathology rather than addressing systemic industrial and pharmaceutical practices.
