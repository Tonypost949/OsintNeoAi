## 1. Executive Summary

A critical cross-jurisdictional investigation has emerged involving the **Orange County Health Care Agency (OCHCA)** and affiliated contractors regarding the systematic diversion of **$10.4 million** in federal COVID-19 relief funds (under the American Rescue Plan Act and CARES Act). According to recent civil filings unsealed in federal court under Case No. **8:2026-cv-00348**, senior county health officials repeatedly dismissed internal ethics warnings and waved off clear conflict-of-interest flags to steer lucrative municipal health and meal distribution contracts to favored non-profit entities and private vendors.

The intelligence analysis indicates severe exposure under the federal False Claims Act (**31 U.S.C. § 3729**) and California Government Code **§ 1090**, which strictly prohibits public officials from being financially interested in any contract made by them in their official capacity. Regulatory agencies, including the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) and the Department of Health and Human Services Office of Inspector General (HHS-OIG), are coordinating parallel inquiries. The systemic bypass of internal compliance mechanisms exposes the involved entities to treble damages exceeding **$31.2 million**, alongside potential criminal liability for mail/wire fraud and public corruption.

## 3. Financial Compliance & Anomaly Breakdown

### Misappropriation of $10.4M COVID-19 Passthrough Relief Contracts
* **Mechanism:** County officials utilized emergency procurement exemptions during the pandemic to bypass competitive bidding, directly routing **$10.4 million** in federal funds to pass-through entities linked to family members and political affiliates.
* **Compliance Deficiencies:**
1. **Suppression of Conflict Warnings:** Executive leadership actively overruled internal ethics memos and compliance officer red flags regarding self-dealing and family affiliations.
2. **Subrecipient Audit Failure:** Complete non-compliance with Uniform Guidance standards (**2 C.F.R. § 200.331**), failing to perform mandatory pre-award risk assessments and post-award single audits on subrecipients.
3. **Inadequate Invoice Substantiation:** Authorization and disbursement of multi-million-dollar wire transfers based on unverified, non-itemized invoices lacking proof of deliverable execution or food service distribution.
