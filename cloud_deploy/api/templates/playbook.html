🇺🇸 NATIONWIDE TAXPAYER ACCOUNTABILITY PLAYBOOK

THE PROBLEM: INCOMPETENCE OR CORRUPTION?
Across the nation, local governments and law enforcement agencies are exposing highly sensitive taxpayer data to the open internet. While this looks like incompetence, following the money often reveals a darker truth: misappropriated funds, shell-company IT contractors, and gross negligence.

When safety regulations prevent citizens from directly auditing what data is bleeding out of these open network ports, we must shift the attack vector from the network to the paper trail.

Here is the exact playbook to hold your city accountable.

STEP 1: SCAN THE PERIMETER (THE RECEIPT)

You do not need to hack a system to prove it is insecure. Using standard, legal OSINT tools (like Nmap, Shodan, or Photon), map the external attack surface of your city's infrastructure.

  • Look For: Open ports on critical infrastructure (Port 3389 for RDP, Port 1433 for SQL, Port 21 for FTP).
  • The Goal: Obtain a hard "receipt" of negligence. If a police department has 40+ open ports, they are objectively failing to secure their network.

STEP 2: FOLLOW THE MONEY (THE IT BUDGET)

If the vault is wide open, who is being paid to guard it?

  • Action: Pull your city's public vendor contracts and budget approvals.
  • Investigate: Identify the IT contractor or managed service provider (MSP) responsible for the network.
  • Cross-Reference: Run the vendor's corporate officers through your state's Secretary of State database. Are they tied to local politicians? Did they receive fraudulent PPP loans? Do they operate multiple shell companies under the same registered agent? (This is known as a Control Cluster).

STEP 3: FILE TARGETED PUBLIC RECORDS REQUESTS (FOIA)

Do not ask for the data inside the server. Ask for the communications about the server.

  • Target: The City Manager, the Chief of Police, and the IT Director.
  • Request: "Produce all Service Level Agreements (SLAs), security audit reports, and internal emails regarding the maintenance, security, and compliance of [City Domain] from [Date] to [Date]."
  • The Trap: If they produce them, you find the negligence in writing. If they deny the request or claim no audits exist, you have proven they are operating blindly.

STEP 4: ESTABLISH LIABILITY AND COMPLIANCE VIOLATIONS

Government agencies are bound by strict data handling laws.

  • Law Enforcement (CJIS): Criminal Justice Information Services compliance mandates strict access controls. Open remote desktop ports (3389) or plaintext database ports are immediate, severe violations.
  • Healthcare (HIPAA): If the city handles health data, open plaintext ports violate federal law.
  • Action: Document these exact violations based on the open ports you found in Step 1.

STEP 5: PUBLISH, PRESSURE, AND REPORT

Take the financial ties, the vendor names, the compliance violations, and the scan receipt, and publish them openly.

  • Local Level: Send the dossier to the city council, demanding an independent audit of the IT vendor's contract.
  • Federal Level: Submit the compliance violations to the FBI's CJIS division or the relevant federal oversight committee.
  • Public Level: Publish the findings on a public transparency dashboard. You don't need the actual data inside the server; the exposure itself, paired with the financial waste, is the weapon that forces accountability.
