# City of Huntington Beach — Citizens' Infrastructure Advisory Committee (IAC) Report & Measure FF Analysis

**Original Document Title:** Final Report and Recommendations of the Citizens' Infrastructure Advisory Committee (IAC)  
**Historical Period:** 1998–2000  
**Legislative Result:** Passage of Measure FF (November 2002 City Charter Amendment § 617(b))  
**Repository Source:** City of Huntington Beach Laserfiche Records / City Clerk Archives  

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Formation Context

In **1998**, following a preliminary 1997 engineering audit indicating that the City of Huntington Beach faced more than **$1.3 billion in deferred maintenance and rehabilitation needs over a 20-year horizon**, the City Council formed the **Citizens' Infrastructure Advisory Committee (IAC)**.

The IAC was tasked with conducting an exhaustive, independent "physical exam" of all municipal infrastructure systems, categorizing deficiencies, evaluating existing funding mechanisms, and proposing dedicated structural solutions for the City Council and electorate.

---

## 2. Core Findings & Infrastructure Valuation (2000 Baseline)

| Metric | Valuation / Finding |
|:---|:---|
| **Total 20-Year Need Identified:** | **$1.37 Billion** (over years 2000–2020) |
| **Primary Driver of Deficit:** | 1960s Annexation Boom (city expanded from 3.5 sq miles to 25+ sq miles; population grew from 11,500 to 172,000 without permanent replacement reserve funds). |
| **Most Critical Systems:** | Stormwater pump stations, wastewater pipelines, arterial streets, and city facilities. |
| **Technology & Data Systems:** | Early supervisory control and telemetry for pump stations and utility networks lacked modern digital redundancy, requiring manual operational workarounds. |

---

## 3. Data & Communication Systems in the 2000 Report

In the 2000 IAC Report, the city's data and technology infrastructure was evaluated across two main categories:
1. **Utility SCADA / Telemetry Controls:**
   * Remote monitoring of the city’s 15+ storm pump stations and wastewater lift stations.
   * Recommendation to automate lift station alarm telemetry to prevent urban flood overflows.
2. **Municipal Records & Public Information Systems:**
   * Transitioning paper engineering maps, parcel records, and council minutes into early electronic document management systems (which later became the on-premise Laserfiche repository).

---

## 4. Legislative Outcome: Measure FF (2002 Charter Amendment)

The IAC's determination that general city revenues were consistently diverted away from preventive maintenance led directly to the drafting and passage of **Measure FF in November 2002**.

### Measure FF Charter Amendment (§ 617(b)) Mandate:
* **The 15% Rule:** Mandated that **15% of all General Fund revenues** must be allocated to the **Infrastructure Fund** for capital improvement projects (CIP), calculated on a five-year rolling average.
* **Prohibited Uses:** Funds in the Infrastructure Fund cannot be redirected to general operational salaries or non-capital operating expenses.
* **Cumulative Impact (2005–2024):** Generated **$697 Million** in capital investment into Huntington Beach infrastructure over the two decades following its passage.
